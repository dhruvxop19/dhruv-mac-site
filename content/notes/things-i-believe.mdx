software used to reward the team that picked one narrow wedge and stayed there long enough to become inevitable.

that was not a bad playbook.

it made sense when shipping real software took huge teams, long roadmaps, expensive infra, and years of patience.

but ai changes the physics.

a small team can now build in weeks, test adjacent ideas fast, and produce with the leverage of an organization ten times its size. the bottleneck is no longer just output. output is getting cheaper every month.

the scarce thing is taste.

judgment. timing. knowing what should exist. knowing what feels obvious only after someone finally builds it. knowing when to ignore a market map and follow the weird pull of a user behavior that should not work but somehow does.

the winners will not just be the teams with the most models, dashboards, or agents.

the winners will be tiny teams with insane taste, moving faster than large companies can coordinate.

## the next era is consumer

the most powerful ai systems in the world are still mostly trapped inside chat boxes, coding tools, enterprise dashboards, and weird empty input fields.

that feels completely wrong.

everyone technically has access to similar intelligence, but almost nobody gets the same leverage from it. the gap is not access anymore. the gap is product.

normal people do not want to learn prompt engineering.

they do not want to configure agent graphs, connect fifteen integrations, debug tool calls, or stare at another blank box asking them what they want to do.

they want things to happen.

they want software that understands the situation, remembers what matters, talks like a person, takes action, and does not make them manage every tiny step.

ai should feel less like operating a machine and more like having a capable extension of yourself.

## distance should collapse

i grew up thousands of miles away from silicon valley.

the internet collapsed that distance for me.

it let me learn to code, find startups, study builders, cold email my way into rooms i had no business being in, and eventually make it to yc.

that still feels unreal.

i think ai can collapse an even bigger distance:

the distance between having an idea and being able to build it.

that is the part i cannot stop thinking about.

i do not want to spend the next decade building products only for the tiny circle of people on tech twitter who already know how to squeeze value out of ai.

i want to build for normal people.

people who do not care which model is running.

people who should never have to know what an agent is.

people who just want better memory, coordination, communication, money workflows, learning, personal admin, and a bunch of categories that probably do not have names yet.

## how i want to build

some experiments will fail.

good. they should.

but the ones that create real pull should get everything.

capital, taste, distribution, infra, energy, obsession, all of it.

that is how i want to operate:

start with a deep technical and product thesis.

explore aggressively around it.

ship fast enough to learn before the market has language for what is happening.

compound every lesson, capability, distribution channel, and piece of infrastructure into the next product.

not a studio throwing random things at the wall.

not a lab hiding behind demos.

a small, dangerous team building consumer ai products with taste.

that is the game.
