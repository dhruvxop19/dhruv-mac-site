i started coding when i was 12 because i liked games.

that was it.

no career plan. no package dreams. no linkedin arc.

just me, game dev, and the insane feeling of making something on a screen obey an idea in my head.

then the usual indian roadmap entered the chat.

high school. jee. engineering college. good package. safe life.

everyone made it sound like the only respectable path, so i followed it without asking if it actually fit me.

i stopped building for fun and started running a race where nobody had explained the finish line.

then i failed jee.

at the time, it felt like the end.

in hindsight, it was the first crack in the script.

after that, i got into aviation. for almost a year, i had enough breathing room to come back to tech. i started learning again, building again, and slowly remembered that i actually loved this before it became a "career decision."

but some part of me still believed college was mandatory.

so i joined NIAT.

it was sold as a new-age tech college. beyond normal engineering. future-ready. all that.

i bought the pitch.

paid the fees.

and then realized it was not the launchpad i thought it would be.

outside college, i was doing work that felt real. building, learning web dev, exploring web3, hacking on ideas, meeting people.

inside college, it felt like friction.

i was getting opportunities way beyond what my tier-3 college could even advertise as its highest package, but when i needed support, there was no NOC, no help, no real backing.

college was not opening doors.

it was standing in front of the ones i had already found.

then i joined X.

that changed everything.

i saw people getting hired because they could ship. building companies without waiting for permission. creating leverage through proof of work, distribution, taste, and speed.

the internet made college feel smaller every week.

not learning.

learning became more important than ever.

but the degree started looking less like a door and more like a receipt.

i wanted to drop out.

my parents obviously did not.

and i get it. for them, college meant safety. status. stability. the thing you hold on to when everything else feels risky.

then i went to bengaluru for a hackathon and met builders who were living in the world i wanted to be in.

people who cared about shipping, not attendance.

proof, not permission.

velocity, not labels.

and then i met [Divya Ranjan](https://x.com/divyaranjan_).

his story hit me in the simplest way:

oh.

this is possible.

i came back and grinded for two months straight.

not to make a dramatic point.

to build enough proof that dropping out looked less like impulse and more like direction.

eventually, it happened.

i dropped out.

not because college is useless for everyone.

not because dropping out is cool.

but because for me, staying had become riskier than leaving.

it was taking time from the thing that was actually working:

building.

shipping.

writing.

selling.

meeting people.

learning through consequences.

so yeah.

that's the story.

just a 12-year-old game-dev kid who got pulled into the roadmap, found the internet, met the right people, and finally chose the thing he actually cared about.
